# NEAR Against Its Peers: an investment thesis

> **Not financial advice.** This file is generated by `make judge-demo` from the committed data snapshot, dated 12 Nov 2026 (`as_of` = 2026-11-12). It is never edited by hand: every number below comes from `data/processed/metrics.json`. Dashboard: https://theodoreroussard.github.io/NEAR-to-its-peers/

## The question

For each dollar of fees paid on-chain, does the market value NEAR more or less than its peers? And which perimeter that includes NEAR Intents can be defended?

The measure is a **multiple**: market cap divided by the fees summed over the trailing 365 days. A lower multiple means the market pays less for each dollar of activity. Fees are the top line, before the cost of token issuance, so this is a price-to-sales ratio, not a price-to-earnings ratio. Subtracting issuance would need data this repository does not compute.

## The claim

Counting the Intents revenue its ecosystem captures, NEAR is valued at 0.33 times the peer median, below its typical peer; like for like, on base-layer fees alone, it is valued at 3.07 times, higher than every peer. On the captured-revenue reading, the Intents revenue behind it held up over the last window, but holders do not yet provably capture that revenue. The investment case rests on the treasury burning or locking what it buys back; until then the strict holder reading is BASE.

> **Why two numbers.** Peers are measured on base-layer fees only. The first number (BASE+NET, 441x) also counts the application revenue NEAR captures through Intents, which no peer gets for its own applications; the second (BASE, 4,072x) is the like-for-like comparison.

**Verdicts: C1, C2 and C3 hold; C4 fires.** The evidence for each one follows.

## Evidence for

- Counting the Intents revenue its ecosystem captures (BASE+NET), NEAR trades at 441x, 0.33 times the peer median of 1,326x: below k = 1.00, so C1 holds. Holding NEAR's market cap and the peer median fixed, C1 would fire only if trailing-year Intents net revenue fell below \$2.39M, a drop of 75%. Counting only Intents revenue booked since the regime break, annualized, the ratio is 0.28 (377x): C1 holds on that reading too.
- Across the panel itself and 7 variants (adding either candidate chain or dropping any one peer), the NEAR BASE+NET ratio moves from 0.32 to 0.51, and the C1 verdict stays the same in every one. The verdict depends on the perimeter, not on the panel: on BASE, like for like, the ratio is 3.07, above k, so C1 would fire on that reading.
- Intents net revenue rose 199%, from \$1.74M over the prior 90-day window to \$5.19M over the last one. C2 fires only on a drop larger than 50%: it holds.
- The share of Intents fees NEAR keeps rose by a relative 41% (+7.97 points), from 19.56% over the prior 90-day window to 27.52% over the last one. C3 fires only on a drop larger than 25%: it holds.
- NEAR's circulating supply grew +2.3% a year, measured from 14 Nov 2025 to 12 Nov 2026, against +0.8% to +20.8% for its peers; 4 of the 5 peers diluted their holders faster.

## Evidence against

- Peers are measured on base-layer fees only, while BASE+NET adds the application revenue NEAR captures through Intents; no peer gets the same credit for its own applications. Like for like, on base-layer fees alone, NEAR trades at 4,072x, 3.07 times the peer median: higher than every peer.
- Over 30-day blocks, Intents net revenue went from \$0.50M (18 Mar 2026 to 16 Apr 2026) to a peak of \$2.28M (14 Sep 2026 to 13 Oct 2026); the latest block, \$1.84M, is below that peak.
- Aptos ranks 104 on CoinGecko on 12 Nov 2026, outside the rule's top 30; it stays in the panel, which was frozen with the thresholds. Without it, the NEAR BASE+NET ratio is 0.32 and C1 holds.
- No burn or lock policy is published for the buyback account, which holds 2,250,000 NEAR; 12,000 NEAR have already left it. The bought-back NEAR can be spent again, so it is not provably held for holders: C4 fires, and the strict holder reading stays BASE (4,072x, 3.07 times the peer median). C4 has two tests: the policy test fires, while the outflow-share test alone passes (0.53% of the holdings, against z = 1%).
- **BASE+NET is an assumption about the treasury.** It counts the Intents revenue the NEAR ecosystem keeps as if it worked for NEAR holders. That is the defended perimeter, stated as an assumption, not a fact.
- **The comparable Intents history is short.** Revenue has been booked daily only since 23 Feb 2026: 263 days of data.
- **Fees reflect pricing as well as activity.** NEAR prices its gas low by design, so a busier NEAR can still collect few base-layer fees. A chain that cuts its prices raises its multiple without becoming less useful.

## What would change my mind

Each criterion is a tripwire. If it fires, that part of the thesis is wrong. The thresholds were frozen in one tagged commit (`thresholds-frozen`) **after these numbers were visible**; the rationale for each one sits next to it in [`config/panel.toml`](config/panel.toml).

| Criterion | Fires if | Threshold | Statistic | Verdict | If it fires |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 Valuation | `NEAR BASE+NET multiple / median(peer multiples) > k (trailing 365 days)` | k = 1.00 | 0.33 | **holds** | Even counting Intents, NEAR is not valued below its peers: the valuation case fails. |
| C2 Durability | `Intents net revenue, last 90 days < (1 - x) * prior 90 days (data after regime_break)` | drop larger than 50% | +199% | **holds** | Intents revenue is not durable: the captured-revenue reading loses its base. |
| C3 Capture rate | `net / gross, last 90 days < (1 - y) * prior 90 days (data after regime_break)` | drop larger than 25% | +41% | **holds** | NEAR's cut of Intents fees is being competed away. |
| C4 Holder accrual | `outflows from the buyback account > z% of holdings, or no burn/lock policy` | z = 1% | 0.53% | **fires** | Holders do not provably capture the revenue: the strict holder reading is BASE. |

**How far each criterion is from firing.**

- **C1:** Holding NEAR's market cap and the peer median fixed, C1 would fire only if trailing-year Intents net revenue fell below \$2.39M, a drop of 75%.
- **C2:** it would fire if Intents net revenue over the last 90 days fell below \$0.87M.
- **C3:** it would fire if NEAR's share of Intents fees over the last 90 days fell below 14.67%.
- **C4:** policy test fires; outflow-share test holds. Publishing a burn or lock policy would leave only the outflow test.

C1 to C3 sit far from their thresholds on this snapshot, and the thresholds were set after the numbers were seen. They guard against a reversal; they do not make the current reading more likely to be right.

What would move the thesis the other way: a published burn or lock policy for the buyback account, with outflows kept below z, would make BASE+HOLDERS (505x) a defensible holder reading instead of BASE (4,072x).

## Limitations

- **Not computed.** The data in this repository cannot support these, so the thesis does not claim them:
  - fully diluted valuations, which locked supply makes non-comparable across chains;
  - a reverse sum-of-the-parts of NEAR's market cap;
  - the application revenue of the peers (the symmetric rule that would give every chain credit for apps that buy back or burn its token);
  - the split of Intents growth between volume and take rate;
  - NEAR's own front-end fees, separated from protocol fees.
- **The valuation test is asymmetric.** C1 compares NEAR with Intents against peers on base-layer fees only, because the peers' application revenue is not computed. A symmetric test would be a new criterion after the freeze, so it is left out rather than added after the fact.
- **The buyback figures are hand-read.** The holdings and outflows behind C4 were read on-chain on 11 Nov 2026 and recorded as sourced constants, not fetched by the pipeline.
- **No multiple over time.** A trailing-year multiple needs two years of daily data, and CoinGecko's free API serves one; the thesis therefore rests on a single date.
- **Ethereum's fees exclude its rollups**, which understates the activity Ethereum anchors.
- **Intents looks more like a cross-chain exchange than a blockchain.** A second panel of cross-chain venues would test BASE+NET from another angle.
- **Supply growth covers 14 Nov 2025 to 12 Nov 2026.** CoinGecko's free API serves about a year of history. Market cap divided by price is a proxy for circulating supply: it includes unlocks and CoinGecko's own supply revisions.
- **One snapshot.** Every figure is dated 12 Nov 2026. `make all` refreshes the data; the verdicts above are recomputed, never typed.
- **DefiLlama attribution.** Fee definitions follow DefiLlama's adapters, linked below, and change when those adapters change.
