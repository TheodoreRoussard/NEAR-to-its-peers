NEAR Against Its Peers

For each dollar of fees paid on-chain, does the market value NEAR more or less than its peers? And which perimeter that includes NEAR Intents can be defended?

Data as of 10 Nov 2026 · trailing 365 days · the thesis · code and data · Not financial advice.

The falsification thresholds (k, x, y, z) were frozen after these numbers were visible. The rationale for each one sits next to it in config/panel.toml.

Market cap per dollar of yearly fees

On base-layer fees alone, NEAR's multiple (4,080x) is higher than every peer. Counting the Intents revenue the ecosystem captures (BASE+NET), it falls to 452x, 0.34 times the peer median: lower than 3 of the 5 peers.

NEAR perimeter NEAR BASE+GROSS (upper bound only) Peer Peer median 1,348x

Method. Multiple = market cap on 10 Nov 2026 / fees summed over the trailing 365 days. Log scale. NEAR appears under its four perimeters; the peer median uses the peers only, never NEAR or the candidate chains. Peers are measured on base-layer fees only: only NEAR BASE is like for like, while BASE+NET also counts NEAR's application revenue from Intents.

Does the result depend on the panel?

C1 holds in every panel variant: the NEAR BASE+NET ratio to the peer median ranges from 0.32 to 0.51, against k = 1.00. The verdict does not depend on which peer is in the panel.

Method. Ratio = NEAR BASE+NET multiple / median of the peer multiples. '+ X' adds a candidate chain to the peers (Tron 91x, Cardano 7,081x); '- X' drops one peer. C1 fires to the right of k.

Source. Panel rule and candidates in config/panel.toml; fees and market caps as above.

Dilution: circulating supply growth

NEAR's circulating supply grew +2.5% a year, against +0.8% to +22.2% for its peers. A chain that issues tokens faster dilutes holders faster, whatever its multiple.
