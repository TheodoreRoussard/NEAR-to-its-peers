NEAR Against Its Peers

For each dollar of fees paid on-chain, does the market value NEAR more or less than its peers? And which perimeter that includes NEAR Intents can be defended?

Théo Roussard · NEAR Hackathon, Investment Thesis track · Data as of 12 Nov 2026 · Not financial advice

Abstract. Counting the Intents revenue its ecosystem captures, NEAR is valued at 0.33 times the peer median, below its typical peer; like for like, on base-layer fees alone, it is valued at 3.07 times, higher than every peer. On the captured-revenue reading, the Intents revenue behind it held up over the last window, but holders do not yet provably capture that revenue. The investment case rests on the treasury burning or locking what it buys back; until then the strict holder reading is BASE.

Key findings

Five questions a skeptical investor would ask, each answered from the data and linked to its evidence.

  1. Like for like, how is NEAR valued?

    Peers are measured on base-layer fees only, while BASE+NET adds the application revenue NEAR captures through Intents; no peer gets the same credit for its own applications. Like for like, on base-layer fees alone, NEAR trades at 4,072x, 3.07 times the peer median: higher than every peer. See the evidence

  2. Counting the revenue Intents brings, how is NEAR valued?

    Counting the Intents revenue its ecosystem captures (BASE+NET), NEAR trades at 441x, 0.33 times the peer median of 1,326x: below k = 1.00, so C1 holds. Holding NEAR's market cap and the peer median fixed, C1 would fire only if trailing-year Intents net revenue fell below $2.39M, a drop of 75%. Counting only Intents revenue booked since the regime break, annualized, the ratio is 0.28 (377x): C1 holds on that reading too. See the evidence

  3. Is the Intents revenue durable?

    Intents net revenue rose 199%, from $1.74M over the prior 90-day window to $5.19M over the last one. C2 fires only on a drop larger than 50%: it holds. Over 30-day blocks, Intents net revenue went from $0.50M (18 Mar 2026 to 16 Apr 2026) to a peak of $2.28M (14 Sep 2026 to 13 Oct 2026); the latest block, $1.84M, is below that peak. The share of Intents fees NEAR keeps rose by a relative 41% (+7.97 points), from 19.56% over the prior 90-day window to 27.52% over the last one. C3 fires only on a drop larger than 25%: it holds. See the evidence

  4. Do NEAR holders capture that revenue?

    No burn or lock policy is published for the buyback account, which holds 2,250,000 NEAR; 12,000 NEAR have already left it. The bought-back NEAR can be spent again, so it is not provably held for holders: C4 fires, and the strict holder reading stays BASE (4,072x, 3.07 times the peer median). C4 has two tests: the policy test fires, while the outflow-share test alone passes (0.53% of the holdings, against z = 1%). Since the regime break (23 Feb 2026), Intents holders revenue equals net revenue every day: all of it funds buybacks. BASE+HOLDERS therefore differs from BASE+NET only by the $1.35M of net revenue booked before the break, which BASE+NET counts and BASE+HOLDERS does not. It is not a separate test of the buyback assumption. See the evidence

  5. How robust is the comparison?

    Across the panel itself and 7 variants (adding either candidate chain or dropping any one peer), the NEAR BASE+NET ratio moves from 0.32 to 0.51, and the C1 verdict stays the same in every one. The verdict depends on the perimeter, not on the panel: on BASE, like for like, the ratio is 3.07, above k, so C1 would fire on that reading. Aptos ranks 104 on CoinGecko on 12 Nov 2026, outside the rule's top 30; it stays in the panel, which was frozen with the thresholds. Without it, the NEAR BASE+NET ratio is 0.32 and C1 holds. See the evidence

Method

  1. Collect the data

    Daily fees and revenue come from DefiLlama, daily market caps and prices from CoinGecko. One snapshot is committed with the code and dated by the last day every series covers, so anyone can rebuild every figure offline.

  2. Compute a multiple for each chain

    Market cap divided by the fees paid over the trailing year. It reads like a price-to-sales ratio: how many dollars of market value the market assigns to each dollar of yearly fees. A lower multiple means the market pays less per dollar of activity.

  3. Compare NEAR with its peers

    The peers are chosen by a written rule, not by hand: smart-contract base chains with a DefiLlama fee series and a top CoinGecko rank. NEAR's multiple is divided by the median of the peers' multiples. Below one, NEAR is valued below its typical peer.

  4. Decide which NEAR fees count

    Most of NEAR's recent revenue comes from NEAR Intents, an application, not from base-layer gas. NEAR is therefore measured under four perimeters, from base-layer fees only to all Intents fees, and the thesis states which one it defends and why.

  5. Set tripwires in advance

    Four falsification criteria (C1 to C4) say what result would prove the thesis wrong. Their thresholds were frozen in a tagged commit, and every verdict on this page is computed from the data, never typed.

  6. Rebuild it yourself

    Run make judge-demo in the repository. It rebuilds the numbers, this page and THESIS.md from the snapshot and runs the tests, with nothing to install and no network.

Terms

Multiple
Market cap divided by the fees of the trailing year.
Peer median
The middle value of the peers' multiples. NEAR and the candidate chains are left out of it.
Ratio to the peer median
NEAR's multiple divided by the peer median. Below one, NEAR is valued below its typical peer; above one, above it.
Perimeter
A definition of which fees count as NEAR's. BASE counts base-layer fees only, like for like with the peers. BASE+NET adds the Intents revenue NEAR's ecosystem keeps: the defended reading. BASE+HOLDERS adds the Intents revenue booked as holders revenue. BASE+GROSS adds all Intents fees: an upper bound only.
NEAR Intents
A cross-chain swap service built on NEAR. Users pay fees, and part of them stays with NEAR's ecosystem.
Regime break
The day from which DefiLlama books Intents revenue daily. Earlier data is not comparable, so the window-to-window tests start there.
Buyback
Intents revenue used to buy NEAR on the market. It reaches holders only if the NEAR bought is burned or locked.
Tripwire
A falsification criterion fixed in advance. If it fires, that part of the thesis is wrong.

Market cap per dollar of yearly fees

Method. Multiple = market cap on 12 Nov 2026 / fees summed over the trailing 365 days. Log scale. NEAR appears under its four perimeters; the peer median uses the peers only, never NEAR or the candidate chains. Peers are measured on base-layer fees only: only NEAR BASE is like for like, while BASE+NET also counts NEAR's application revenue from Intents.

Source. DefiLlama and CoinGecko. NEAR: fees API, methodology, market cap; Solana: fees API, methodology, market cap; Ethereum: fees API, methodology, market cap; Avalanche: fees API, methodology, market cap; Sui: fees API, methodology, market cap; Aptos: fees API, methodology, market cap.

Which NEAR fees count

Method. Every perimeter is a trailing 365-day sum ending on 12 Nov 2026, divided into NEAR's market cap on that day.

Source. DefiLlama, NEAR Intents: revenue, fees, holders revenue, methodology.

Does the result depend on the panel?

Method. Ratio = NEAR BASE+NET multiple / median of the peer multiples. '+ X' adds a candidate chain to the peers (Tron 90x, Cardano 7,016x); '- X' drops one peer. C1 fires to the right of k.

Source. Panel rule and candidates in config/panel.toml; fees and market caps as above.

NEAR Intents revenue, and the share NEAR keeps

Method. Whole windows ending on 12 Nov 2026, none before 23 Feb 2026: revenue is booked daily only since then, so earlier data is not comparable. C2 compares the last window's net revenue with the prior one's; C3 compares their net / gross shares, the part of Intents fees NEAR keeps.

Source. DefiLlama, NEAR Intents: revenue, fees, holders revenue, methodology.

Do the buybacks reach holders?

Method. Outflows are transfers out of the buyback account since the first buyback. A burn or lock policy would make the bought-back NEAR provably unspendable.

Source. On-chain account: nearblocks.

Dilution: circulating supply growth

Method. Circulating supply = market cap / price, compared at the start and the end of the period, then annualized.
